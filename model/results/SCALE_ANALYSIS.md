# Scale analysis: does feasibility depend on size, or on shape?

Generated by `model/scale_analysis.py` from committed calibration and
mission CSVs. See `model/README.md` for the laws and `paper/SCALING_LAWS.md`
§8c for the argument.

## The question

Every committed PIC run is a Ø10 mm body. The paper's mission table is a
Ø10 mm chipsat. Neither is a useful spacecraft. If the concept only works
at gram scale it is a curiosity; if the feasibility condition is
scale-free it is a propulsion option for CubeSats.

## Assumptions (engineering inputs, not measurements)

The power source is not part of the thruster. Body-mounted solar cells
appear here only as a worked example of a supply that scales with
skin area; any other skin-areal source changes the margin, not the
size-cancellation.

- solar cells 30% efficient, 25% orbit-average illumination → 102 W/m² of cell area
- cells cover 33% of skin (rest is bare collector + structure)
- drive 100 V (F/P ∝ 1/√V, so the frontier's low end)
- drag area S_ref = ram face + (C_d,side/C_d) × walls parallel to the flow,
  C_d = 2.2, the orbit sims' bookkeeping (C_d,side/C_d = 0.031 at 500 km)
- mass at 3U density (1.33 g/cm³); it enters only the
  sink rate of Step 6, never the thrust demand

## Step 1: demand and supply are both areal

Drag charges for the drag area S_ref: the ram face plus grazing friction
on the walls parallel to the flow. Any body-mounted supply pays from the
skin (solar cells as the example). Thrust demand, hence current, hence
power, all scale with S_ref, so power per unit S_ref is a property of
altitude and drive alone:

| altitude | drag (mean) | P required |
|---|---|---|
| 400 km | 394.9 µN/m² | **1367 W per m² of S_ref** |
| 500 km | 91.1 µN/m² | **315 W per m² of S_ref** |
| 550 km | 46.1 µN/m² | **160 W per m² of S_ref** |
| 600 km | 24.0 µN/m² | **83 W per m² of S_ref** |

Harvest per unit skin area is likewise size-free. Size cancels from
both sides. What survives is the shape ratio skin/S_ref.

## Step 2: closure by shape, at every size

Margin = example supply / demand. The point is that the ratio is
size-free; the absolute number is secondary. The wall term makes
S_ref larger than the ram face, most on long end-on bodies:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| squat can O10x5.5mm (measured) | 0.79 cm² | 1.07 | **3.8** | 0.01 W | _0.1×_ | _0.4×_ | _0.8×_ | 1.5× |
| slender O10x30.5mm (measured) | 0.79 cm² | 1.38 | **10.2** | 0.04 W | _0.3×_ | 1.1× | 2.2× | 4.2× |
| 1U cube, face-on | 1e+02 cm² | 1.12 | **5.3** | 2.04 W | _0.1×_ | _0.6×_ | 1.1× | 2.2× |
| 3U end-on (10x10x30 cm) | 1e+02 cm² | 1.37 | **10.2** | 4.76 W | _0.3×_ | 1.1× | 2.2× | 4.2× |
| 6U end-on (10x20x30 cm) | 2e+02 cm² | 1.28 | **8.6** | 7.49 W | _0.2×_ | _0.9×_ | 1.8× | 3.5× |
| 12U end-on (20x20x30 cm) | 4e+02 cm² | 1.19 | **6.7** | 10.89 W | _0.2×_ | _0.7×_ | 1.4× | 2.8× |

The slender chipsat and the 3U CubeSat return near-identical margins
because they have near-identical shape ratios. Feasibility is a shape
property.
The 400 km demand exceeds the example supply at every size. Supplying
it is a mission-design question, and it too is size-free.

## Step 4: collection gets easier with size

Bare thermal collection is `I = A_skin · j_the`, validated to ±1 % at rung
`collector.thermal`. At the committed plasma row j_the gives:

| body | skin | I_thermal | boost needed @600 km | regime r/λ_D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| squat can O10x5.5mm (measured) | 3.17 cm² | 0.005 mA | 15.0× | 2.5 |
| slender O10x30.5mm (measured) | 11 cm² | 0.016 mA | 5.6× | 2.5 |
| 1U cube, face-on | 600 cm² | 0.882 mA | 10.6× | 25.4 |
| 3U end-on (10x10x30 cm) | 1.4e+03 cm² | 2.058 mA | 5.6× | 50.9 |
| 6U end-on (10x20x30 cm) | 2.2e+03 cm² | 3.235 mA | 6.6× | 61.1 |
| 12U end-on (20x20x30 cm) | 3.2e+03 cm² | 4.705 mA | 8.4× | 71.3 |

The chipsat must run at χ ≈ 150–320, deep in the extrapolated
enhancement regime. A 3U needs only a few × over bare thermal, so its
float depends on the ±1 %-validated thermal flux plus a small correction.
Larger bodies need less extrapolation, not more.

## Step 5: the caveat, a different sheath regime

Every committed run is at r/λ_D ≈ 2.5. CubeSat radii are
25–60 λ_D, where the OML exponent does not apply at all: the sheath is
thin and grows with φ, so enhancement is an area ratio rather than an
orbital-motion fan. Using that (Child-sheath) model instead gives an
estimate, not a calibration, since no run sits in this regime:

| body | altitude | boost | φ estimate | float tax at 100 V |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1U cube, face-on | 500 km | 40.1× | 40 V | 40% |
| 1U cube, face-on | 550 km | 20.3× | 23 V | 23% |
| 1U cube, face-on | 600 km | 10.6× | 13 V | 13% |
| 3U end-on (10x10x30 cm) | 500 km | 21.0× | 59 V | 59% |
| 3U end-on (10x10x30 cm) | 550 km | 10.6× | 32 V | 32% |
| 3U end-on (10x10x30 cm) | 600 km | 5.6× | 16 V | 16% |
| 6U end-on (10x20x30 cm) | 500 km | 24.9× | 87 V | 87% |
| 6U end-on (10x20x30 cm) | 550 km | 12.6× | 48 V | 48% |
| 6U end-on (10x20x30 cm) | 600 km | 6.6× | 25 V | 25% |
| 12U end-on (20x20x30 cm) | 500 km | 31.8× | 130 V | 130% |
| 12U end-on (20x20x30 cm) | 550 km | 16.1× | 73 V | 73% |
| 12U end-on (20x20x30 cm) | 600 km | 8.4× | 39 V | 39% |

The float is a tax on the drive, not a gate (no float value is a design
limit): the 3U's 16 V float at 600 km costs 16% of a 100 V drive,
its 59 V float at 500 km costs 59%, and the minimum-power operating point moves to a
higher drive (SCALING_LAWS §2: optimum near V = 2φ). Unmeasured, but the
direction is favourable, and a large-body PIC run is inexpensive: lower χ
settles faster than anything already run.

## Step 6: mass sets the tolerance, not the demand

Holding altitude takes thrust equal to the drag force, and the drag force
has no mass in it, so none of the numbers above depend on mass. Mass
decides what happens while thrust falls short of drag (drag peaks above
capability, duty-cycled operation): the craft sinks at
da/dt = 2aF/(mv) until thrust catches up. Sink rate at the mean drag
with the thruster off:

| body | mass | ballistic coeff. @500 km | 500 km | 550 km | 600 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| squat can O10x5.5mm (measured) | 0.58 g | 3 kg/m² | 2071 m/day | 1060 m/day | 559 m/day |
| slender O10x30.5mm (measured) | 3.2 g | 13 kg/m² | 481 m/day | 247 m/day | 130 m/day |
| 1U cube, face-on | 1.33 kg | 54 kg/m² | 120 m/day | 61 m/day | 32 m/day |
| 3U end-on (10x10x30 cm) | 4 kg | 133 kg/m² | 49 m/day | 25 m/day | 13 m/day |
| 6U end-on (10x20x30 cm) | 8 kg | 142 kg/m² | 45 m/day | 23 m/day | 12 m/day |
| 12U end-on (20x20x30 cm) | 16 kg | 153 kg/m² | 42 m/day | 22 m/day | 11 m/day |

Mass grows with volume and drag with area, so the ballistic coefficient
grows with size: at equal density a 3U sinks about 10× more slowly than
the slender can for the same shortfall. This is the one quantity that is
not scale-free, and it favours the CubeSat.

## Conclusion

The measured device is a Ø10 mm can, but the result is not about a
Ø10 mm can. Demand and supply are both areal, so the feasibility
condition reduces to a shape ratio and an altitude. CubeSat-class craft
in their natural end-on configurations sit at more favourable shape
ratios and less extrapolated collection physics than the chipsat that
was measured. The 400 km demand is scale-free too: it exceeds the
example body-mounted supply at every size.
